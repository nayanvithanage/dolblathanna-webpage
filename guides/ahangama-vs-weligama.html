Ahangama vs. Weligama: Where Should You Stay?

South Coast, Sri Lanka • 7 min read

We get this question a lot, and we'll be upfront about our bias — we're an Ahangama property. But we've sent plenty of guests over to Weligama for a day, and plenty of Weligama-based travellers have asked us why they didn't just stay here instead. Here's the honest comparison, not the sales pitch.

The short version

Weligama is bigger, busier, and better suited to complete beginner surfers thanks to its long, gentle bay. Ahangama is quieter, has a more local, less touristy feel, and suits surfers who want a mellow but slightly more interesting wave, plus travellers who want a base that doesn't feel like a resort strip. They're 20–25 minutes apart, so honestly, a lot of visitors end up experiencing both regardless of where they sleep.

Surf, in more detail

Weligama Bay's big advantage is its shape — the bay blunts incoming swell into small, forgiving, rolling waves that are genuinely ideal for someone standing up on a surfboard for the first time. It's the reason Weligama has become such a hub for surf schools and beginner camps. If this is your first-ever surf trip, Weligama's wave is arguably the better one to learn on.

Ahangama's home break is a right-hand point that holds more shape and rewards a bit more skill, while still being forgiving enough for a confident improver. For those wanting something with real punch, Midigama's reef breaks are a short ride from Ahangama and offer more consequential waves for experienced surfers — see our surf season guide for details. In short: total beginners often prefer Weligama's wave; everyone else tends to prefer having Ahangama's variety on their doorstep.

Atmosphere

This is where the towns diverge most. Weligama has grown fast in the last decade and has the infrastructure to show for it — more hotels, more restaurants, more nightlife, and correspondingly more of a "surf town on the tourist trail" feel, especially along its busiest stretches. Ahangama has grown too, but more slowly and in a way that's kept a lot of its local character — fishing boats still work the same beaches surfers use, and the pace overall is calmer. Neither is objectively better; it depends whether you want energy or quiet.

Food

Weligama has more restaurants in absolute terms, reflecting its size. But Ahangama's food scene has grown into something genuinely good in its own right — a cluster of quality cafes serving the international surf crowd alongside solid local rice-and-curry spots. Getting between the two

The two towns are close enough that staying in one and visiting the other for a day is completely normal — a 20–25 minute tuk-tuk or scooter ride, or a short train hop. Plenty of our guests base themselves with us in Ahangama and take a morning trip to Weligama for a beginner surf lesson, then come back for a quieter evening. See our transport guide for the practical details.

Our honest take: if this is your first surf trip ever and you want the biggest range of beginner-friendly schools in one place, Weligama has an edge. If you've surfed before, want a quieter base with easy access to a range of wave types, or just prefer a more local feel to your evenings, Ahangama is the better fit — and you can still get to Weligama in twenty minutes whenever you want it.

Whichever you choose, you're not locking yourself out of the other — this stretch of coast rewards exploring beyond wherever you sleep.
