Airport Transfer Guide

South Coast, Sri Lanka • 4 min read

Bandaranaike International sits well north of the South Coast, and the drive down — 2.5 to 3 hours depending on traffic — is most visitors' first real taste of Sri Lankan roads. It's not a leg to wing on arrival: sorting a transfer in advance means a driver is actually there waiting for you, not something you're negotiating for at 11pm after a long flight. Here's how to get that first stretch right.

Already planning to book? Real prices and availability for a Colombo airport transfer are here — arrange your driver before you fly.

Your real options

  • Private transfer, booked in advance. A driver meets you in arrivals with your name, a fixed price agreed before you land, and a direct drop-off at your South Coast accommodation. The default choice for most visitors, and the one this guide recommends.
  • Train from Colombo Fort. Cheaper and genuinely scenic, but it means getting from the airport into Colombo first — a taxi or bus leg you'd need to arrange separately — before the train itself even starts. Better suited to a later leg of your trip than the first hour after landing.
  • Tuk-tuk or taxi on arrival. Technically possible but a rough way to start a long-haul trip — you're negotiating a fare with a queue of touts right after getting off a plane, with no fixed price agreed in advance.

Our honest recommendation: book a private transfer before you fly. The price difference over a tuk-tuk/taxi negotiated on arrival is small, and what you get in return — a driver who's actually expecting you, a fixed price, and 2.5–3 hours to sleep or watch the coastline instead of manage logistics — is worth it after a long flight.

What to expect on the drive

Budget 2.5 to 3 hours from Bandaranaike International to the Ahangama/Weligama/Galle area, depending on traffic through Colombo. Most transfers run in a private car or van, direct to your accommodation, with no stops or shared rides. It's a genuine door-to-door option — no changing trains, no working out which bus goes where with jet lag setting in.

Practical tips

  • Book before you fly, not after you land — arranging a transfer in advance means a fixed, agreed price and a driver who's actually expecting you.
  • Confirm your flight details when booking so your driver can track a delay and adjust the pickup time.
  • Keep your driver's contact details accessible on your phone in case arrivals gets confusing.
  • Once you've landed, see our Getting Around guide for how to get around day-to-day once you've reached the coast.

Of everything to sort before you fly, this is the one that most directly affects how the first few hours of your trip actually feel — worth booking in advance rather than leaving to chance.
