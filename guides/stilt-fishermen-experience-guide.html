Stilt Fishermen Guide

Koggala & the Ahangama coast • 3 min read

Photo: Jakub Hałun, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Stilt fishing is one of Sri Lanka's most recognisable images — a fisherman balanced on a pole driven into the reef, rod in hand. It's a real tradition along this stretch of coast, particularly around Koggala and the Ahangama coastline, and worth knowing the honest context before you go looking for it.

Honest context first: today, much of what visitors see is arranged specifically for photos rather than active subsistence fishing. It's still a genuine local tradition and a striking sight — just go in with the right expectations, and expect a small fee if you want a posed shot.

How to actually see it

There's no single standard way to book this. Most visitors see stilt fishermen as a stop within a wider tour — both the Kosgoda turtle hatchery tour and the Galle tuk-tuk loop pass real stilt fishermen along their routes. In fact, the Kosgoda tour and this one are the same real 6-stop tour — it also covers Galle Fort, a moonstone mine, a lake safari, and a tea estate in one day, if you want something more structured than a roadside stop.

Should you pay for a photo?

A small fee for a posed shot is standard practice and generally expected — it's how many fishermen along this coast now earn from the tradition, alongside or instead of active fishing. It's a fair, transparent exchange rather than something to avoid.
